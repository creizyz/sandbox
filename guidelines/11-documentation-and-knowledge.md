# Documentation and knowledge

Use these standards when creating or maintaining durable knowledge. The organization of bootstrap,
skills, guidelines and project context is defined in
[AI-assisted projects](01-ai-assisted-projects.md). Project documentation owns the concrete placement
map, file naming conventions, tooling and migration status.

## Ownership and selection

Give each rule or contract one authoritative home. Link from other subjects rather than maintaining
copies that must agree. Documentation near code or assets should describe that subject; cross-cutting
material needs a project-wide owner. Avoid placing operational commands in reusable standards.

Keep indexes useful for selection: state the question answered, scope and when a reader should
consult a page. Link to relevant local indexes without copying their inventories. Do not require
readers to traverse empty directory layers or read every page before identifying applicability.

Each page should have a coherent subject and intended audience. Split when responsibilities or
selection needs differ, rather than according to an arbitrary size. Supporting examples, images,
data and measurements stay with the explanation and have explicit provenance where relevant.

## Status and evidence

Distinguish requirements, accepted decisions, current implementation, proposals, research and
historical records. A description of current limitations needs a defined scope; avoid extending
a legacy component's restriction to an entire system. State assumptions and unresolved questions.

For significant decisions, retain context, alternatives, rationale, consequences, status and any
required approval. Preserve why a decision was made without treating it as permanently optimal.
For configuration values and measurements, identify their source, version and whether they are
defaults, shipped settings, examples or observed results.

Use traceable evidence and maintain enough context to interpret it. External links should support
the nearby claim; internal links should lead to the authoritative source. Respect attribution and
licensing for copied material. A citation does not convert a proposal into an accepted decision.

## Updates, migration and history

Update descriptions when the behavior or rule they own changes. Maintain incoming links, outgoing
relative links, indexes and useful heading anchors when moving material. Prefer a short transition
link to a second full copy. Record remaining migration work explicitly rather than implying that
moving an index has moved all its targets.

Archive superseded or inapplicable material with a status banner and a link to its replacement
where one exists. Age alone is not a reason to archive a still-valid contract or rationale.
Keep historical records identifiable and prevent them from being mistaken for current instructions.

Check links and structure, then inspect meaning and discoverability. A successful link checker
does not establish accuracy, authority or actual agent retrieval. Evaluate consequential routing
with representative tasks as described in [AI-assisted projects](01-ai-assisted-projects.md).
