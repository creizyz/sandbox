# Research and evidence

Use these standards when acquiring knowledge or supporting a decision with external or experimental
evidence. A research skill supplies the procedure; this guideline defines the quality of its result.

## Questions and applicability

State the question, relevant definitions, scope and decision the evidence should inform. Identify
assumptions that could alter the conclusion. Distinguish descriptive questions about what exists,
causal questions about effects and normative choices about what should be preferred.

Match evidence to the claim. Implementation behavior is best established by inspected code and
observations under stated conditions; a language guarantee by the applicable specification;
a scientific claim by suitable research. Peer review is useful evidence of scrutiny, not a
universal reason to rank an unrelated paper above a directly applicable authoritative source.

## Source quality

Prefer original specifications, documentation, datasets, studies and reproducible measurements.
Use secondary explanations when they clarify or synthesize, while tracing consequential claims to
their basis. Record version, date, population, environment or jurisdiction when applicability depends
on them. Verify time-sensitive claims against current sources.

Assess methods, sample selection, baselines, uncertainty, conflicts of interest and independent
replication where relevant. Multiple pages repeating one source are not independent confirmation.
Do not equate popularity, a vendor assertion or an anecdote with comparative evidence.

Represent contrary findings fairly. Explain whether disagreement comes from methods, definitions,
conditions or genuine uncertainty. Absence of a discovered result is not proof of absence.

## Traceability and conclusions

Place citations near the claims they support. A source must substantiate the specific assertion;
do not use a broad link to give an inference the appearance of external authority. Label direct
observations, source claims, deductions and recommendations distinctly. Quote accurately and
sparingly; preserve qualifications that matter to interpretation.

Explain the strength and limits of conclusions. Do not generalize a benchmark beyond its workload
or infer causation from association without justification. Numerical precision should reflect the
measurement and model, not merely the number of digits a tool prints.

Make investigations reproducible to the extent appropriate: retain source identifiers, relevant
versions, inputs, methods and transformations. Record unresolved questions and what evidence could
settle them. Stop when further investigation is unlikely to change the decision at its required
confidence; do not turn a bounded engineering task into an unrequested literature survey.

For experiments, use [Performance and measurement](08-performance-and-measurement.md) or
[Verification and testing](07-verification-and-testing.md) as appropriate. Communicate the result
according to [Reasoning and communication](02-reasoning-and-communication.md).
